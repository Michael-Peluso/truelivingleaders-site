The bottleneck

Every company has one link that sets its pace.

A business is a chain. You win the work, estimate and sell it, deliver it and collect the money. People and decisions hold every link together. Eliyahu Goldratt showed in The Goal that a chain's output is set by its slowest link. Improve anything else and output barely moves. Improve the slowest link, and the whole company speeds up.

Past $5M, that link is usually the way decisions are structured.

Most companies past $5M have outgrown the structure that built them. Nobody did anything wrong. The company grew faster than the way it makes decisions, so pricing, hiring, the change order and the schedule still route through one seat. When they do, the company can only grow as fast as one calendar allows.

That's why True Living OS starts with the CEO's time and the leadership team around it. Move each decision to the leader ready to own it, and the company grows past the old limit. Then the bottleneck moves, and we work on the next one.

How we work

Map. Design. Install. Grow.

Working on the business is a process. Each pass clears one bottleneck, and the process repeats when the bottleneck moves. Every company moves through it at its own pace.

Goldratt's five focusing steps, and the part of our process that carries out each one

1 · Map

The Growth Map: we find the bottleneck.

Every engagement starts with the Growth Map, a defined first step with a clear finish line. It's Goldratt's first step, identify the bottleneck. We start with understanding your business, in the same order an investor would, and you leave with the map, the bottleneck named and the plan to clear it.

  1. Time with you. Your goals, and where your hours actually go.
  2. An hour beside each leader. Watching how the work really moves.
  3. The numbers. Read the way an investor would read them.
  1. 01Financial diligence.

    Your statements, margins, cash and backlog. This becomes your baseline scorecard.

  2. 02Front of the funnel.

    Where your work comes from, who brings it in, and how much of it depends on one person.

  3. 03Throughput.

    How work moves through sales and management, and where it slows down before a decision.

  4. 04The team doing the work.

    Who delivers for your customers, and what they need from the office to keep the work moving.

AIHow the onboarding agent helps: Once your tools are connected, the onboarding agent reads your numbers and helps our team build the baseline scorecard and the bottleneck map. Our team walks through every finding with you.

What you get

  • A time map. Where the CEO's hours go, sorted into work only the CEO can do, work a leader is ready to own, and work a process or an agent can handle.
  • A baseline scorecard built from financial diligence.
  • A bottleneck and leadership map. Where work and decisions slow down, and which leaders are ready to own more.

What you commit to

  • Open books. Financial statements, plus job cost reports and a WIP schedule if you run projects.
  • Time with our team, and time for our team with every person on yours.
  • Straight answers, including the uncomfortable ones.

2 · Design

We design the ecosystem around the bottleneck.

Design covers Goldratt's next two steps. Get the most out of the bottleneck, then line everything else up behind it. When decisions route through one seat, that means protecting the CEO's time for the work only the CEO can do and building the parts that give every other decision a clear owner.

What gets designed

  • The scorecard. Which numbers, who owns each one, and where each number comes from.
  • The lanes. Who owns what, with written decision rights and dollar limits.
  • The guardrails. What your team decides, what your leaders decide and what comes to the CEO, with limits set each quarter.
  • The rhythm. When decisions get made and followed up, built around protected time for the work only you can do. We cut meetings that don't decide anything.
  • The processes to write down first, starting with the handoffs where work stalls on the swimlane map.
  • The agents to install, and the tools to connect.

What you commit to

  • Decide what only you should keep. The list is usually shorter than owners expect.
  • Agree the decision rights in writing, including the ones that feel uncomfortable to hand over.

3 · Install

We put it in with your leaders, one piece at a time.

Install is Goldratt's fourth step, elevate the bottleneck. We add decision-making capacity across the leadership team, so the company moves at the pace of the whole team.

What goes in

  • The scorecard goes live, and your leadership team reviews it every week.
  • Your leaders take their lanes. Our team holds a weekly working session with you and up to two of them.
  • The calendar installer puts the rhythm on every leader's calendar, including your protected time.
  • The meeting agents gather what each leadership session needs and follow up on every decision.
  • The SOP builder drafts SOPs for each lane, for the leader who owns it to review and approve.
  • An on-site session where we work through the swimlanes and handoffs with your leaders.

What you commit to

  • Let two leaders own real outcomes.
  • Let your leaders make the calls you've handed them, including the ones you would have made differently.
  • Bring decisions into the rhythm, where your team can see how they get made.

4 · Grow

The bottleneck moves. We find the next one.

Grow is Goldratt's fifth step, repeat. With the CEO's time freed and leaders deciding in their areas, the company grows, and the bottleneck moves to sales, estimating, delivery or the front of the funnel. That's the problem you want, because it can be solved with people, process and capacity. We map the new bottleneck and go again.

What goes in

  • The quarterly session sets growth targets and the plan to reach them, on site or on an Executive Retreat.
  • Your leadership team runs the scorecard and the rhythm.
  • We score the company's value when we start and again a year in, and compare.

What you commit to

  • Set the bigger goal.
  • Let the system carry the decisions it was built to carry.

Guardrails

Clear limits, set each quarter. No surprises.

Each quarter, the CEO and the leadership team set limits on spending, pricing and hiring, and a floor for cash. Inside the limits, leaders decide in their areas with current numbers in front of them. Questions about True Living OS.

What is a management ecosystem?

The connected system that lets a company run and grow with every leader deciding in their own area. Clear numbers on a scorecard, leaders with clear lanes and decision rights, a rhythm for decisions and follow-up, written processes and AI agents doing the gathering and follow-up. Each part feeds the others. True Living OS is our team's installed version of it.

Why start with the bottleneck?

Because every company has one link that sets its pace, and fixing anything else barely moves the company. Past $5M, that link is usually the way decisions are structured, not the people making them. We clear it first, then find where the bottleneck moved and work on that.

How long does it take?

It's a process. Map, Design, Install and Grow, then again when the bottleneck moves. Each company moves through it at its own pace, and we'll talk about yours on the qualifying call.

Will this add a lot of meetings?

No. The rhythm gives every decision a set time and place, so fewer meetings decide more. We cut meetings that don't decide anything, protect time for the work only the CEO can do, and hand the gathering and follow-up to the agents.

What is executive lag?

The time between a problem being raised and a decision being made. Shrinking it is one of the fastest ways a company grows. The A in TEAM shrinks it: AI gathers the updates and the numbers, so the leader who owns the call can make it at the next session.

Why do you start with the financials?

Because that's where an investor starts. The numbers show what the company actually does, and they become your baseline scorecard. Everything we build after that gets measured against them.

What does our CFO or controller get from it?

Every number on the scorecard has one owner and is current every week, so cash, margin and receivables are seen early. Limits on spending, pricing and hiring, and a floor for cash, are set each quarter, and anything above them arrives prepared. The financials become the baseline everything else is measured against.

What do our key leaders get, and sign up for?

Clear authority over their area, real-time numbers and AI support. In return, a weekly working session with our team, their part in the rhythm and ownership of their numbers on the scorecard. Up to two of them work alongside the CEO from the start, so they help build the system they'll be running.

Which tools do the agents connect to, and who sees our data?

QuickBooks, Google Sheets, your CRM and email are the usual starting points. You choose what to connect.

Does it work outside construction?

Yes. Construction is our featured industry, and our founder owns and operates construction businesses. The bottleneck, the scorecard, the lanes and the agents work the same way in any established company doing $5M or more a year.

What happens at the quarterly session?

You choose. Our team comes on site for an intensive with your leadership team, or the CEO takes The Rubicon, an Executive Retreat in Moab, to reset, see the business from the outside and come back with the vision and the plan for the next level.

What happens when the first bottleneck is cleared?

The bottleneck moves, and we decide together what comes next. Often that means another pass on the new bottleneck. Sometimes your leaders run it from there. We base the call on the results.

Do you promise results?

We won't promise a revenue number, a valuation or an exit date. Results depend on you and your team doing the work. We will install the system, and we measure the company's value when we start and again a year in so you can see what changed.
